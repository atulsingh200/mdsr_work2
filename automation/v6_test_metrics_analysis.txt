  Interpretation:
    Essentially zero correlation — more training samples do NOT reliably
    predict better test accuracy. This confirms that the bottleneck is
    content distinguishability, not data volume. Pairs like T7-T8 have
    max training data (1,500) but perform worst (0.41), while T1-T14 has
    minimal data (70 train) but achieves perfect accuracy.

    The negative sign is driven by T14 pairs: they have low train counts
    AND low accuracy, but many high-train-count pairs also have low accuracy
    (T7-T8, T4-T5, T10-T11), creating noise in the correlation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. KEY FINDINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. OVERALL: 82.3% accuracy, 81.5% macro — solid but with clear weak spots

  2. MONOTONIC GAP TREND: Accuracy increases with tier gap (0.75 at gap=1
     to 1.00 at gap=14), confirming the model learns real ordinal signal

  3. T14 IS A BLACK HOLE: 0.52 per-tier accuracy, 6 of bottom 10 pairs
     involve T14. With 1 doc and 7 segments, it's statistically unlearnable.
     Removing T14 pairs would boost overall macro significantly.

  4. T7 IS SYSTEMATICALLY CONFUSED: 0.70 per-tier accuracy despite 6 docs.
     T7 content (channel configuration) overlaps with T8 (messaging) and
     T11 (reporting/monitoring). T7-T8 at 0.41 is the worst non-T14 pair.

  5. TRAINING DATA DOESN'T FIX CONTENT OVERLAP: Zero correlation between
     train count and test accuracy. The hard pairs (T7-T8, T4-T5, T10-T11)
     have maximum training data but remain near random.

  6. ADJACENT PAIRS ARE HARDEST: Macro 0.75 for adjacent vs 0.82 for
     non-adjacent. 4 of 14 adjacent pairs are at/near random.

  7. T1 AND T2 ARE TRIVIALLY SEPARABLE: Perfect 1.00 accuracy across all
     pairs — foundational/introductory content is lexically distinct.

  8. WHACK-A-MOLE PATTERN: Within the T6-T11 cluster, improving one pair
     tends to degrade another across training runs, suggesting shared
     vocabulary forces the model into trade-offs in embedding space.

  Analysis:
    v6 IMPROVES micro (+6.2pp) but DEGRADES macro (-5.1pp).

    Micro improves because v6 does much better on high-volume pairs:
      T5-T6 (+10.7pp, n=320), T6-T7 (+35.3pp), T8-T9 (+25.1pp)
    These large test-set pairs dominate the weighted average.

    Macro degrades because v6 loses several pairs badly:
      T4-T5 dropped from 1.00 to 0.50 (-50pp)
      T10-T11 dropped from 0.82 to 0.54 (-28pp)
      T3-T4 dropped from 0.98 to 0.75 (-23pp)
      T13-T14 dropped from 0.75 to 0.50 (-25pp)

    Root cause: v5 trained on adjacent pairs only (each pair ~10% of data).
    v6 trains on all 105 pairs — each adjacent pair is only ~1.4% of data.
    The all-pairs training teaches coarse ordinal signal well but dilutes
    fine-grained adjacent discrimination.

    The whack-a-mole pattern is visible across versions:
      v5 strong, v6 weak: T4-T5, T3-T4, T10-T11, T13-T14
      v5 weak, v6 strong: T6-T7, T8-T9, T5-T6
    Different training distributions cause different pairs to win/lose.
